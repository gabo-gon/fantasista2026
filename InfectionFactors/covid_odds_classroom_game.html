What did we learn?

Two stages, one story: avoiding infection and reducing the chance of severe illness are related but different questions.

🛡️
Stage A: Exposure & infection

Vaccination and well-fitting masks can help reduce risk, but they do not make infection impossible. Handwashing is less central for a virus that mainly spreads through the air.

❤️
Stage B: Illness severity

Age, certain health conditions, and vaccination status are associated with the risk of severe disease. Population-level associations do not predict one person's outcome.

✨
Placebo effect value

For lifestyle factors without a proven direct effect in this simplified model, a tiny +0.001 “placebo” bonus is used purely for the game. It is not a real protective effect.

Research notes
  • Williamson et al. (2020), Nature, “Factors associated with COVID-19-related death using OpenSAFELY.” doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2521-4
  • Hippisley-Cox et al. (2021), BMJ, QCOVID3 risk prediction after vaccination. doi:10.1136/bmj.n2244
  • Buitrago-Garcia et al. (2020), PLOS Medicine, asymptomatic and presymptomatic SARS-CoV-2 infections. doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1003346
  • Mohammed et al. (2023), Vaccines, systematic review of vaccine effectiveness against Omicron. doi:10.3390/vaccines11020224

These studies cover different populations, variants, periods, and endpoints. Their estimates cannot simply be combined into one clinical calculator.
